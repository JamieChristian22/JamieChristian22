## What makes a bounty genuinely payable?

A GitHub issue is not automatically a funded bounty. Please supply:

1. The public issue/repository and identity of the authorized project owner or payer.
2. A specific amount or payment terms, with verifiable funding or escrow if offered.
3. The rules for claiming the task, competing submissions, review and acceptance.
4. The expected technical scope, licensing of contributions, and deadlines.
5. A confirmed payout process, supported payment location and eligibility requirements.

The developer should confirm these terms **before starting**. A project may reject work even if a PR is submitted, and no payment is assumed unless the agreed conditions are met. I do not publish confidential company information or accept work involving unauthorized access.

For example, projects may fund scoped contributions through an established bounty or contracting platform; [Algora](https://algora.io/) supports contracts and funded GitHub issue work. Its presence here is a research lead, **not** a claim that I have an account, an accepted bounty, or an existing partner relationship.

## Maintenance proposals for a company

A sensible initial engagement can be **one small, verifiable milestone**:

- **Discovery and scoping:** confirm rights, dependencies, and success criteria.
- **Deliverable:** implement the agreed documentation, tests, or maintenance change in a reviewable branch/PR.
- **Acceptance:** validate agreed checks and document any failures or limitations.
- **Payment:** according to mutually accepted milestones and written terms.
- **Optional retainer:** only after a completed milestone and a separately agreed support scope, capacity and service-level terms.

No 24/7 support, guaranteed vulnerability response, security certification, or uptime commitment is offered by default.
