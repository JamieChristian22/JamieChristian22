## Safe path to company-funded maintenance and bounties

1. Seek a **verifiably funded** issue or a direct written proposal from a person authorized to commission the work.
2. Confirm scope, repository ownership, security boundaries, milestone criteria, payout method, rights and acceptance process.
3. Do not imply that a public project's `good first issue` label or sponsor link is a paid bounty.
4. Consider contracted support only for software that is actually maintained and used; educational portfolio repositories do not create corporate maintenance obligations.

## Metrics — keep your financial numbers private

Once a week, privately record: date, GitHub followers, relevant repo stars/forks, sponsor count, inbound project inquiries, accepted paid work, and money actually received. Keep client rates, tax details, personal sponsor information and revenues **out of public repositories**. Use your own private spreadsheet or notes to monitor outcomes and avoid treating follower count as proof of paid demand.
